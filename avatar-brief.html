Kira Avatar — Design Brief

Defines what the avatar needs to express and why, ahead of final art/animation production. See the Lantern concept in motion on the dining mockup.

1. What the avatar needs to express

Five states, design and animate as one continuous character, not five disconnected poses:

StateGuest-facing meaningDesign note
IdleAmbient, waitingSubtle looping motion (breathing/blink-style), not static — must never look frozen/broken
Wake"I'm listening to you now"Clear, fast perk-up — the moment a guest decides the tablet heard them
ListeningActively hearing speechMust read clearly even with a live subtitle overlapping it
ThinkingProcessing (covers the response-latency window)Must not look frozen — the state most likely to get a second tap from an impatient guest
SpeakingDelivering a responseLip-sync not required for pilot — a simpler talk-cycle is fine as long as it's visibly distinct from idle

Every state must also hold up with reduced text size on, and with a subtitle bar present — design with the subtitle area reserved, not added after.

2. Visual direction — open, not yet decided

No house style exists yet. Two directions to choose between:

  • Abstract/orb-like (glowing shape, light, particles) — hides the lack of a "face," ages well, easiest to animate smoothly, lowest risk of looking uncanny in any state.
  • Character-like (stylized face or creature, non-photoreal) — more memorable/brandable, but much higher design and animation cost to get all five states + transitions reading well.

The dining mockup explores an abstract direction — "the Lantern" — drawing on the hanging-lantern motif found in Gulf hospitality interiors rather than generic sci-fi-assistant iconography. Recommend presenting both directions as quick concepts before committing; this is a branding decision as much as a UI one.

3. Technical constraint the design must respect

The on-prem box's GPU is already running the AI model and speech processing. A heavy real-time 3D render competes for that same hardware and adds latency on top of an already-tight response-time budget.

Recommendation: design as a 2D rigged/vector character (Rive or Lottie export), not a 3D model.

  • Build it as layered vector art with a skeletal rig, not a pre-rendered video loop — so state transitions can blend smoothly rather than hard-cutting.
  • Lip-sync to TTS is out of scope for pilot — design a generic "talking" motion rather than phoneme-accurate viseme animation.
  • Deliver source files (Rive .riv or AE/Lottie .json) sized for a tablet-resolution canvas, not print/marketing resolution.

4. Deliverables expected from this brief

  • Two quick concept directions (abstract vs. character) — low-fidelity, for a go/no-go decision.
  • Once direction is picked: the five states above as a rigged animation, exported Rive/Lottie.
  • A static "hero frame" per state, for dropping into the hi-fi mockups.

5. Open question

Does the client have an existing brand mark/color palette the avatar should align to, or is Kira's visual identity being created fresh alongside this?
